The Map

Emergencies keep breaking out across 40 towns — 15,000 people, and every emergency has a deadline. Six crews respond on foot. The dispatcher's picture of the region refreshes only every 20 minutes, so the desk is always working from a slightly old map. The question: when a fresh map lands and a crew is already walking somewhere, do you turn them around? Blue: people being served. Orange: waste — ground walked back over, or a deadline missed. Watch where the orange ends up.

Chase the map
Replans like SLIME MOULD — regrow toward whatever looks richest right now. Every fresh map, every crew turns from wherever it stands, and the road behind them re-ignites orange: cleared ground, walked back over, logged as waste.
Reroute at the tips
Commits like a TREE — the trunk never regrows, only the twigs. The town a crew is walking toward stays locked; only its later stops may change. The threads run long and clean — watch who pays for that.

The lessonThe dispatch desk that turned its rescue crews around every time a newer map arrived wasted 82 km of walking and still reached almost every emergency before its deadline. The desk that kept its crews on their planned routes wasted nothing and let ten emergencies expire. Acting on new information saved more people than sticking to the plan, every time we tested it.
