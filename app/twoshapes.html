The Two Shapes

A disaster region: 64 communities, 16,299 people. Software survey agents visit every community and file reports, but the reports must pass through four summary stages — each with limited room — before reaching the people who send help. Both halves of this screen show the same region receiving the exact same visits, tick for tick. The only difference is the rule deciding which reports keep their place at each stage. Blue: a community the decision-makers can still see. Orange: reports being dropped, and communities disappearing from view.

The lessonThe survey visits were identical in both halves, so the only difference was the rule deciding which reports keep their place at each summary stage. That rule alone decided which communities the decision-makers could see: 26 of 64 communities vanished from view while still being visited. Sending extra surveyors to the vanished communities fixed nothing, because their new reports were dropped at the same stage as the old ones. Changing the rule so neighboring communities' reports merge and travel together brought all 64 back at no extra cost.
