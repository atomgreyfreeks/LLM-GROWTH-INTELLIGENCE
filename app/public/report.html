How we keep the results honest

Predictions are saved publicly before anything runs, and scripts count every score.

  • Every world is generated from a committed seed, and the same seed produces byte-identical event logs, asserted on every run.
  • Exactly one rule differs between compared runs — the growth rule under test — and budgets are matched and asserted in code.
  • The organizing rules are enforced mechanically by the harness. The AI agents only read, judge, and choose.
  • Scoring is a script run against an answer key sealed before the experiment. Nobody grades by opinion.
  • Thirteen of our own pre-registered predictions have been refuted so far, and every refutation is published beside the survivors.

Seven of the eight have been through real experiments. The organizing rule runs as harness machinery; the agents do the thinking.

The finding, and the request

Growth rules decide the outcome when the budget binds. We want the compute to find exactly where each rule stops being safe.

What we found at small scale. Where a rule truly forced the agents' hands, the simulated failures replicated exactly with real agents. Where any slack remained, capable agents rescued every structure. The choice of organizing rule is cheap insurance that pays out precisely when capacity is scarce.

What scale unlocks. Thresholds — the exact budget where each rule flips from asset to liability — and the monoculture question: do seven institutions sharing one model fail together, where seven genuinely different model families catch each other?

~1,000,000
agent decisions planned: fleets of 100 to 300 agents, 5 to 10 rounds, 3 to 5 rules, 10 seeded trials, about 25 budget configurations
