The Boat

A storm is flooding a coast: 48 towns, 18,000 people, one rescue boat. Two kinds of report arrive about every town — a satellite view, and phone calls from the ground — and the storm blinds the satellite and cuts the phones exactly where it floods. Both desks receive the identical reports; the only difference is where the two streams meet. Blue: rescue. Orange: time burned on doubt.

One map
Blends like a BACTERIAL SWARM — every report dissolves into one shared signal field, and the boat follows the strongest scent. The swarm's known weakness is herding: a wrong signal steers everyone, and on this desk doubt has no place to live.
Go and look
Pairs the two witnesses like a LICHEN — two different organisms kept distinct so they can disagree. When they split about a town, the boat first flies out to check, a ROOT TIP going for the missing fact. Every check spends rescue time.

The lessonBlending the satellite pictures and the phone calls into one map worked fine, right up until the phone calls became unreliable — then that desk had no way to tell which part of its map to distrust. And the desk that flew its boat out to double-check every conflict between the two sources saved the fewest people of all, because the checking flights used up time the boat should have spent rescuing. Double-checking needs its own budget.
